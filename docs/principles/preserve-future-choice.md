# Preserve future choice

<p class="cf-lede">Prefer openness, portability, interoperability and explicit trade-offs.</p>

<div class="cf-panel cf-panel--principle"><strong>Principle</strong>Prefer openness, portability, interoperability and explicit trade-offs.</div>

## Why this matters

Architecture should preserve strategic freedom where that freedom has value. Lock-in can be justified, but it should be understood rather than accidental.

## What this means in practice

Use this principle to make the decision smaller, clearer and easier to revisit as evidence changes.

## Questions to ask

- Can we move the data?
- Are interfaces based on open or widely supported standards?
- What would leaving this supplier or platform require?
- What benefit are we receiving in return for lock-in?

## During a Test & Learn Experiment

Do not over-engineer portability for a short-lived experiment, but avoid creating irreversible dependencies before the evidence justifies them.

## Watch out for

- Treating all proprietary technology as bad
- Pretending switching costs do not exist
- Building abstraction layers with no plausible need
